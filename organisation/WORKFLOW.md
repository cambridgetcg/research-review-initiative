# One case, from question to correspondence

Proposed process · no case is approved or message authorised by this document.
Use one copy of [CASE-TEMPLATE.md](CASE-TEMPLATE.md) per case. Keep the process
small enough that the evidence can be checked by a recipient.

| Step | Concrete output | Condition for moving on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Select | Exact claim, source version, why it matters, conflicts and budget. | A technical question is identified; a notice or suspicion alone is insufficient. |
| 2. Preserve | Source/evidence inventory with locators, access dates, hashes, rights and gaps. | Another reviewer can identify the same inputs; unavailable material is explicit. |
| 3. Specify | Short protocol with assumptions, check, controls, tolerances, resources and stopping rule. | Scope is fixed before results; amendments will be dated and explained. |
| 4. Examine | Proof analysis or computation, observations, failed attempts and limits. | Distinguish derivation, supplied-code reproduction and separately authored implementation. |
| 5. Check | Scoped second review, exact reviewed version and conflict/controller declaration. | Missing independence is disclosed; it is never filled by another account of the same operator. |
| 6. Invite reply | A short private query to the relevant author, with the exact draft and evidence. | Actual sender, recipient and message are reviewed before sending; record delivery separately from a reply. |
| 7. Decide | Revised report, response or disagreement, contributor approvals and editor's reasons. | Each conclusion is supported at its stated scope; unresolved issues remain explicit. |
| 8. Communicate | Recipient-specific cover note, report, public-safe evidence, requested action and delivery record. | The destination is relevant and verified from its official channel; no bulk escalation. |
| 9. Maintain | Reply log, correction/appeal record and version links. | Never silently erase prior attribution or treat silence as agreement. |

## Methods minimum

For mathematics, name the proposition, quantifiers, domain, assumptions and exact
step under review. Give a derivation or explicit counterexample. Numerical
checks support a bounded observation; they do not prove a universal statement.
Record formal verification separately from informal proof review if used.

For computation, retain code, data provenance, software/environment, random seeds,
configuration and a runnable command. Specify metrics, tolerances and controls
before running. Record all runs in the declared grid and explain failures or
exclusions. Verify intermediate quantities when final metrics could conceal a
different implementation. Separate a transport/hash check from a numerical
replication and from an independent assessment of the model and assumptions.

For either, write the result as the smallest supported claim. “Not reproduced
under this protocol” and “disproved by this counterexample” are different
conclusions. Neither establishes intent. An editorial or metadata issue may
leave a mathematical result intact; reassess particular dependencies rather
than marking an entire citation graph false.

## Reply and recipient routing

Use a proposed **14-calendar-day initial reply window**, adjustable for scope,
availability or an agreed extension. This is an operating target, not a legal
deadline. State the actual date in the message. If a public notice requires
different handling, record the specific reason. Never send a report solely
because this template's clock elapsed.

Contact the corresponding author or relevant method/artifact maintainer about
technical questions. A journal editor is appropriate for a correction to that
journal's record or a methods/reproducibility submission. An institution's
designated research-integrity office is a different route, requiring a concrete
institution-relevant concern; ordinary proof disagreement is not automatically
an integrity complaint. A research group may be invited to give a scoped review.
Check each recipient's actual remit and submission instructions before use.

Choose one relevant initial destination rather than distributing accusations to
many institutions. Keep public addresses sourced from official pages; do not
harvest private contacts. Do not use a recipient's logo or list them as a partner
because a message was sent or acknowledged. Preserve confidential replies and
record agreed quotation/publication terms. A report may fairly summarise a
response and remaining disagreement without suggesting the author endorsed it.

### Author-query draft — unsent

**Subject:** Technical question about [exact result and source version]

Dear [verified recipient],

We are [actual initiative/status and accountable sender], with [funding and
relevant affiliations]. We examined [precise claim]. Under [assumptions/protocol],
we found [bounded observation, without inferred intent]. The draft and
reproduction materials are [versioned locators/digests].

Could you clarify [one or two concrete questions] or identify a missing
assumption, artifact or correction? We propose [actual reply date], and can
discuss an extension. No response will be treated as confirmation. Please tell
us which parts of a reply may be quoted in a later report. Our proposed next
step is [specific destination or publication], subject to review of your reply.

[Real sender, role and functioning reply channel]

We submit the attached [version/date/digest] for [technical assessment,

performed by [actual contributors/controllers]; remaining limitations and
conflicts are [summary]. The author was contacted on [date]; [accurate response
status] is retained in the report. We do not infer misconduct from this result.

The evidence packet provides [proof/command, artifacts, controls and versions].
Please confirm the appropriate route or tell us what further information is
needed. This submission does not imply your endorsement or any agreed action.
